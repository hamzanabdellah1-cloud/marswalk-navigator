# Demo script

About five minutes. Open `index.html`. Leave the ribbon visible.

## 1. Place two points

"Click a start and a destination."

Click the orbital image twice, or use the guided demo, which places two points on cells under 8°. S and D appear. The hint tells you what the next click does.

## 2. Three lines from the elevation grid

"The app finds several routes from elevation data."

Shortest, Gentlest slope, and Most science interest are separate cards when the paths differ. On the sample pair used by the demo, Shortest is about 580 m and Gentlest slope is about 720 m, with a lower steepest cell. Read the trade-off sentence. It is built only from those numbers.

If two presets match, one card says "same as Shortest".

## 3. Move the sliders

"Change the sliders and watch the trade-offs move."

The slope limit is yours. It is not a NASA or crew limit. Raising it can open cells the lines could not enter. "Avoid steep ground" and "Favour science terrain" recompute the custom line. If it matches a named line, the note says so.

## 4. Open a science stop

"Open a science stop."

Open the boxwork close-up. It is a Curiosity photo from May 16, 2025, with ChemCam and CheMin named by role only. The second photo is May 2025 only. An "Ex" marker is an example on this frame, not a claim that the rover stood on that cell.

## 5. What the data supports

"Here is what the data supports, what it does not, and why."

The confidence list is yellow for the elevation grid, the science-interest score, and the pathfinding model. The grid is about 3 m, from the 1 m Gale DEM. The page does not know soil traction, rocks at walking scale, or whether a stereo artifact is in the DEM. The orbital picture is older than the 2025 photos. Science interest is a terrain heuristic.

We bring different Mars observations together to help explain route choices and prepare for science, not to certify a route as safe.
